Linus is a software architect and systems engineer best known for creating the **Linux operating system kernel** and the **Git distributed version control system**.

He studied computer science at the University of Helsinki, where he began tinkering with Intel 386 hardware, task switching, and POSIX terminal drivers—work that eventually grew into one of the largest collaborative engineering projects in human history.

His engineering practice is centered on **systems taste**: the belief that truly elegant architecture does not invent complicated layers of abstraction, but instead structures data so cleanly that edge cases disappear entirely. He works primarily in C and assembly, with a relentless focus on minimizing latency, preventing regressions, and keeping code close to the metal.

Beyond operating systems, his creative pursuits include prime-lens documentary photography, underwater optical studies, technical drafting, and building sovereign, minimalist digital workstations.
